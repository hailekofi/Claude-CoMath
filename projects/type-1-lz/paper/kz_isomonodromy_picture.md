This note expands the one-line claim that "P is an isomonodromy/KZ object, generically Kampé de
Fériet." It (1) explains what an isomonodromy/KZ problem is, (2) defines the Kampé de Fériet
function, and (3) shows concretely how this machinery *supports a solution* for the Type-1 N=3
transition amplitudes — i.e. it is a constructive route to a closed form in named special
functions, with the elementary cases (BE, special slices) as degenerations.

Throughout, the established facts about our model are: `i dψ/du = (H₀ + uA)ψ`; the ring/commuting
structure gives a shared eigenbasis `φ_i(u)` and the adiabatic-frame generator
`G^(a)(u) = diag(E^(a)(u)) − iW(u)` with `W_ij=⟨φ_i|φ_j'⟩` **a-independent** and `E^(a)` **a-linear**;
the spectral curve is `u = n(λ)/p(λ)`, `p(λ)=∏(λ−ε_i)`, with the phase one-form
`ω = √Q₄·L_H·W₄/p³ dλ` and window periods `I_X = ∮ω` (the assay's objects).

## 0. The reframing: P is the Stokes data of a meromorphic ODE

`i dψ/du = (H₀+uA)ψ` is a linear ODE whose coefficient matrix is a degree-1 polynomial in `u`.
At `u=∞` it therefore has an **irregular singular point of Poincaré rank 2**: solutions behave like
`exp(−i(H₀u + ½u²A))` (Gaussian/parabolic-cylinder growth). The N=2 case is *exactly* Weber's
parabolic-cylinder equation, and the celebrated LZ factor `e^{−πΔ²/v}` is its **Stokes multiplier**.

So the LZ transition matrix is, precisely, **connection + Stokes data of an ODE with an irregular
singularity at ∞**. `P_{ij} = |S_{ij}|²` where `S` glues the asymptotic solution basis at `u→−∞`
to the one at `u→+∞` across that singularity. Everything below is machinery for computing that `S`.

---

## 1. What is an isomonodromy / KZ problem?

**Flat connections and monodromy.** A linear ODE with rational coefficients,
`dY/dλ = M(λ)Y`, `M(λ)=Σ_i A_i/(λ−ε_i)` (Fuchsian: simple poles at the `ε_i`), defines a *flat
connection* `∇ = d − M(λ)dλ` on the punctured sphere. Continuing a solution around a pole `ε_i`
multiplies it by a **monodromy matrix** `M_i`; the tuple `(M_1,…,M_n)` (a representation of
`π₁` of the punctured sphere) is the **monodromy data**. When there is also an irregular point
(here `∞`), one adds the **Stokes matrices** there. *The transition matrix `P` lives in exactly
this data.*

**Knizhnik–Zamolodchikov (KZ).** The KZ equations (Knizhnik–Zamolodchikov 1984, from 2D conformal
field theory) are the statement that a particular rational connection is *flat as the pole positions
move*. With the poles at `z_i` and a Lie algebra `g`,
```
κ ∂Ψ/∂z_i = Σ_{j≠i}  Ω_ij/(z_i − z_j)  Ψ ,        Ω_ij = split Casimir of g on factors i,j.
```
The residues `H_i = Σ_{j≠i} Ω_ij/(z_i−z_j)` are the commuting **Gaudin Hamiltonians**; their joint
diagonalization is the Bethe ansatz. Flatness ⇔ the "infinitesimal braid relations"
`[Ω_ij+Ω_ik, Ω_jk]=0`. **Our Cauchy/Gaudin data is of exactly this type:** poles at the `ε_i`,
residues built from the couplings `γ`. This is the content of "our `H₀+uA` is the same
Cauchy/Gaudin data" — and it is why BBGY can map integrable LZ to KZ.

**Isomonodromy.** The *dual* notion: deform the parameters of the ODE (pole positions, or here the
slopes/spectral data) while keeping the monodromy + Stokes data **constant**. The deformation
equations are nonlinear (Schlesinger equations for Fuchsian systems; Jimbo–Miwa–Ueno for irregular
ones); the Painlevé equations are the simplest examples. The key bridge to our problem:

> **Chernyak–Sinitsyn's "`τ`-invariance of the transition probabilities" *is* isomonodromy.**
> Their `τ`-deformation moves the model parameters while leaving `P` unchanged — i.e. it preserves
> the Stokes/monodromy data. Because it is generated by a *commuting* partner (Abelian), it is a
> *linearizable* slice of the full (generally nonlinear) isomonodromic deformation: it shuffles the
> data without computing it. This is the precise reason the commuting partner cannot, by itself,
> *produce* `P` (the Abelian ceiling) — it only certifies which deformations leave `P` fixed.

**Why this is the right home for our problem.** The ring structure showed `P^(a)` = holonomy of a
*fixed* connection `W` twisted by *a-linear* phases. "Fixed rational connection + linear-in-a twist"
is precisely a KZ/Gaudin connection with the slopes `A` as the spectral/twist parameter: `W` carries
the `Ω_ij`-residues (a-independent, built from `γ,ε`), and the a-linear diagonal is the
irregular-type data at `∞`. The wavefunction, as a function of `u` *and* the parameters, satisfies a
KZ-type linear system; its `u→±∞` asymptotics are `P`.

## 2. What is a Kampé de Fériet function?

It is the standard **two-variable generalization of the hypergeometric function** `₂F₁`. With
Pochhammer symbols `(a)_n = a(a+1)…(a+n−1)`,
```
F^{p:q;r}_{l:m;n} [ (a_p):(b_q);(c_r) ; (α_l):(β_m);(γ_n) ; x,y ]
   = Σ_{s,t ≥ 0}  [ ∏(a_i)_{s+t} · ∏(b_j)_s · ∏(c_k)_t ]
                  ────────────────────────────────────────────  ·  x^s y^t /(s! t!) .
                  [ ∏(α_i)_{s+t} · ∏(β_j)_s · ∏(γ_k)_t ]
```
The defining feature is that some parameters enter through the **coupled** index `s+t` (the "shared"
Casimir directions) and others through `s` or `t` **separately** (the two individual channels). It
contains:
- the four **Appell functions** `F₁…F₄` as low-order special cases;
- a **product/`₂F₁` reduction** when the coupled parameters drop out — this is the *elementary/known
  special-function* corner;
- **double Euler integral representations** `∬ ∏(…)^{(powers)} dx dy`, which is how it arises from
  the KZ contour integrals.

For N=3 the solution space is rank 3 and the connection has **two independent cross-ratios / two
"gaps"** (the two avoided crossings = the two `Q₄` windows). A rank-3 KZ/Gaudin connection with
three marked points `ε_i` and two essential moduli produces precisely a **two-variable**
hypergeometric object — a Kampé de Fériet function (BBGY obtain exactly this in their App. A.2; the
2×2 case there is an ordinary `₂F₁`). The **two arguments are built from the two window actions**.

## 3. How this *supports a solution* for the amplitudes (the constructive pipeline)

This is the point: the KZ/isomonodromy picture is not just a label, it is a recipe. Five steps.

**(i) Integral (Euler/Laplace) representation.** The Cauchy/Gaudin structure gives the wavefunction
an explicit contour-integral form
```
ψ(u) ≈ ∫_Γ  exp( i·u·ℓ(λ) ) · ∏_i (λ − ε_i)^{α_i} · R(λ)  dλ ,
```
where `ℓ(λ)` is the linear-in-`λ` phase set by the slopes `A`, the **exponents `α_i` are fixed by
the couplings** (they are exactly the BE/Dykhne weights `α_i ∝ Γ_ij`), and `R` is algebraic. This
is the multi-state version of Lin–Sinitsyn's `I = ∫ x^α (x−iβ₁)^{−1+ξ₁}(x−iβ₂)^{ξ₂}dx` (their
Eq. 11), which already produces `Beta × ₂F₁` for one gap. The exponent in the integrand is the
assay's phase action; the saddle points of `i u ℓ(λ) + Σα_i log(λ−ε_i)` are the **complex turning
points = the `Q₄` window branch points**.

**(ii) Bases at the two ends = choices of contour.** The asymptotic solution bases at `u→±∞` are
realized by specific steepest-descent contours `Γ` threading the saddles. As `u` rotates through the
irregular singularity at `∞`, the dominant saddles switch — this is the **Stokes phenomenon** — and
the admissible contours jump.

**(iii) Connection coefficients = ratios of period integrals.** The matrix `S` that re-expands the
`u→+∞` basis in the `u→−∞` basis is built from **ratios of the contour integrals over a basis of
cycles** of the spectral curve `μ²=Q₄`. The *diagonal* (survival) pieces are the closed window
periods `I_X` — these collapse to residues `2πi Σ Γ_ij` (the assay's result) and give the
**elementary BE survivals**. The *off-diagonal* (interference) pieces are the **relative periods /
the contour that runs between the two windows** — the genuinely two-window object.

**(iv) Evaluate → Kampé de Fériet.** For N=2 there is one saddle pair: the single integral is a
Beta/Gamma function and you get the elementary LZ formula. For N=3 there are **two saddle pairs**
(two windows) and the connection coefficient is a **double integral over the two cycles** → a
**Kampé de Fériet function of two variables**, whose arguments `x,y` are functions of the two window
actions (equivalently the two reduced gap/coupling ratios). The **middle-survival prefactor we
measured (2.5–104×) is exactly the value of this Kampé de Fériet factor** — the non-Abelian
holonomy of `W` that the commuting partner cannot see.

**(v) Probabilities and degenerations.** `P_{ij} = |S_{ij}|²`. The **elementary corners** are
controlled degenerations of the Kampé de Fériet function: (a) extreme-level survivals → closed
window periods → residues → BE (exact, elementary); (b) the `ε_CS=0`-type / decoupling slices →
the coupled parameters drop out → `₂F₁` or product reduction → elementary. The **generic middle
survival** is the irreducible two-variable value — closed form, but transcendental.

## 4. What this buys, honestly

- **A concrete object, not a dead end.** "No elementary closed form" is *not* "no closed form." The
  amplitudes are connection coefficients of an explicit KZ/Gaudin flat connection, with an Euler
  integral representation whose exponents (`∝ Γ_ij`) and saddles (`Q₄` windows) we already have. The
  N=3 answer is a **Kampé de Fériet function of two window-actions**; `P=|·|²`.
- **The right division of labor (from the ring structure).** Abelian data (commuting partner /
  WKB / Dykhne) → the exponents and the BE survivals, exactly. Non-Abelian data (the KZ connection
  coefficient) → the Kampé de Fériet prefactor = the middle survival + off-diagonals.
- **A constructive program.** Write the Euler representation for Type-1 N=3 explicitly; identify the
  cycle basis of `μ²=Q₄`; compute the connection matrix by steepest descent across the two windows;
  read off the Kampé de Fériet arguments; verify `P=|S|²` against the `10⁻⁹` harness; recover BE and
  the slices as degenerations.

## 5. Status / caveats

- BBGY establish the KZ ↔ integrable-LZ map and the Kampé de Fériet N=3 solution for the
  **hyperbolic** model `Â+B̂/t` (regular singularity at `0`, irregular at `∞`). Our **linear**
  model `H₀+uA` is the **confluent** case (the two ends are joined through a *single* rank-2
  irregular point at `∞`). Confluence is standard but not free: it can merge singularities and
  introduce extra Stokes structure, so the exact connection constants must be redone for the linear
  case — this is the real technical work, and the one place a surprise (e.g. an extra transcendental
  Stokes constant, as the frame-map test warned) could still appear.
- The claim "rank-3 + two moduli ⇒ Kampé de Fériet" is the expected/generic outcome and matches
  BBGY; for special Type-1 sub-loci it can degenerate to `₂F₁` or elementary. Which locus our
  benchmark strata sit on is checkable.
