\begin{abstract}
We restate the goal --- a \emph{closed-form, computationally practical} expression for the
$3\times3$ Type-1 multistate Landau--Zener (MLZ) transition matrix $P$, reducible to the core
parameters $\{\gamma_i,\eps_i,a_i\}$ with \emph{no} numerical ODE integration and at most a
handful of geometric (Abelian) integrals --- and rank-order the available strategies against
that bar. The two extreme-level survivals are already exact (Brundobler--Elser, realized as the
imaginary window periods $I_X$); the open content is the middle-level survival $\Pmid$ and one
independent off-diagonal. \textbf{This revision (v2) incorporates the four primary sources, now
read in full}; they sharpen both the ceiling and the route. The decisive finding is
authoritative and sobering: Chernyak--Sinitsyn (arXiv:2006.15144), analysing \emph{exactly} our
linear $3$-state class with its time-quadratic commuting partner, state that for nonzero
middle-level energy $\eps_0\ne0$ ``there is likely no analytical solution of the general
three-state LZ model''; and that the multistate generalisation of the Dykhne complex-turning-point
formula --- which is precisely what our $Q_4$-window machinery \emph{is} --- ``has no general
analog'' for $N=3$, with only ``limited progress'' on the subleading prefactor. That prefactor is
exactly our missing $\Pmid$/off-diagonal. We therefore \textbf{re-rank}: the
\textbf{zero-curvature / Lax path-deformation route} (Malikis--Cheianov, arXiv:2505.06048) is
promoted to first, because it is the \emph{only} method among the four with demonstrated
\emph{elementary} closed-form output (an exact factorisation $S=\prod S_{ij}$ of two-level
S-matrices), and it cashes out the commuting-partner lever directly. The KZ/Gaudin route (BBGY,
arXiv:2409.17053) is demoted: it is \emph{hyperbolic} ($\hat A+\hat B/t$) and its $N{=}3$ output is
a two-variable Kamp\'e de F\'eriet function --- it tells us the \emph{transcendentality class} of
the off-diagonal rather than delivering an elementary form. We give five ranked strategies with
falsification tests, and an honest reset of the practicality bar.
\end{abstract}

\paragraph{Status legend / evidence ladder.}
\textsc{established} (proof or overwhelming combined evidence) $\succ$
\textsc{analytically-derived} $\succ$ \textsc{numerically-supported} ($\approx$ near-proof, the

\section{The problem and the practicality bar}
%==============================================================
The model is $H(u)=H_0+uA$, $A=\mathrm{diag}(a_i)$, with the Cauchy/Type-1 coupling
$(H_0)_{ij}=\gamma_i\gamma_j(a_i-a_j)/(\eps_i-\eps_j)$. The observable is the doubly-stochastic
$P_{x\to j}=|f_j(+\infty)|^2$, with $4$ free real parameters. Type-1 matrices form a
\emph{commuting family} (the integrability structure). Known exactly:
\begin{equation}
P[\text{extreme-slope level survives}]=\prod_m e^{-2\pi\Gam_{nm}},\qquad
\Gam_{ij}=\frac{\gamma_i^2\gamma_j^2\,|a_i-a_j|}{(\eps_i-\eps_j)^2},
\label{eq:BE}
\end{equation}
the Brundobler--Elser (BE) law, here realized intrinsically as $|\!\Im I_X|=2\pi\sum\Gam_{nm}$
where $I_X=\oint_{\gamma_X}\omega$ is a window period of the phase one-form on
$E:\mu^2=Q_4$. \textbf{Open:} the middle survival $\Pmid$ and one independent off-diagonal.

\paragraph{Why the bar matters.} The BE result already meets the bar: the elliptic period $I_X$
\emph{collapsed to residues}, giving the elementary $2\pi i\sum\Gam_{ij}$ with no surviving
elliptic integral. The transport residues are even topological ($\pm i/4$, \texttt{transport\_actions.py}).
The open part must be sought in objects that admit the \emph{same} residue/period collapse ---
not in machinery whose output is an unresummed series.

\item \textsc{established} --- \textbf{Phase exponent} $D_{2m+1}(H)$ closed to all orders via

\paragraph{The one local gap, and why it is \emph{not} the lever.}
The lone remaining \emph{local} symbolic gap is the all-orders transport recurrence
$\Theta^{\rm tr}_v(t)$ (only $K_1=-A_2/2A_1$, $K_3$ explicit). \textbf{But closing it does not
deliver a practical amplitude}: $\Theta^{\rm tr}_v$ is one factor (the scalar block
$s^{\rm BS}_v=-i\,e^{2\Theta_v}$) at \emph{one} ramification point, returned as a power series in
the Langer coordinate that still needs Borel resummation, then assembly across all active selector
points and multiplication by sector propagators. Under the practicality bar it is a low-leverage
finishing detail, not a route to closed form. This is the key strategic correction from the audit.

\section{The structural lever: commuting family + guaranteed crossing}
%==============================================================
Two facts should drive the attack.
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,leftmargin=1.4em]
\item \textbf{Slope-linearity of the phase.} In the gauged adiabatic ODE
  $f_i'=-\sum_{j\ne i}S_{ij}f_j+i(E_i-E_x)f_i$, the coupling $S_{ij}$ depends only on
  $(\gamma,\eps)$ (transport data); the slope vector $a$ enters \emph{only} through the diagonal
  phase $E_i=\sum_j a_j\gamma_j^2/(\lambda_i-\eps_j)$, \emph{linearly} in $a$. (This is exactly
  why $D_{2m+1}$ is affine-linear in $L_H$.) So $P=P(a)$ is generated by a \emph{fixed} geometric
  transport plus an $a$-linear phase --- a strong constraint the closed form must respect.
\item \textbf{Guaranteed exact crossing $\Rightarrow$ a regular singular point.} A commuting
  partner $H'$ (same $\gamma,\eps$; different slopes $a'$) shares the eigenvector geometry; the
  guaranteed level crossing is a degeneracy point whose \emph{local monodromy is computable}. In
  the zero-curvature picture (S1) this is precisely a marked point at which the deformed path
  reduces to a two-level LZ vertex with residues built from $\{\gamma,\eps,a\}$.
\end{enumerate}
\textbf{Caveat (falsified).} The lever must \emph{not} be cashed out as a product of two-level
factors --- that is the falsified \texttt{integrability.py} route (the benchmark off-diagonals
imply a \emph{non-symmetric} $\Gam'$, so no partner slope can satisfy a two-Dykhne product). Its
only viable use is as a flat-connection / conserved-quantity \emph{constraint} system.

\section{Findings from the four primary sources (read in full)}
%==============================================================
The four papers form a coherent narrative that resets expectations.
\begin{itemize}[nosep,leftmargin=1.4em]
\item \textbf{Chernyak--Sinitsyn 2021 (arXiv:2006.15144) --- our exact model class.} Their
  general $3$-state $H_3=\mathrm{diag}(b_1t,\eps,-b_2t)+\{g_{ij}\}$ \emph{is} Type-1; the
  commuting partner is \textbf{time-quadratic} (their Eq.~20), the integrability condition is
  $|g_{ij}|^2/(B_{ii}-B_{jj})=|\gamma_{ij}|^2$ conserved plus an ``area-conservation rule.''
  \emph{Verdict:} integrability $\Rightarrow$ $P$ is invariant under a $\tau$-deformation but
  ``\emph{generally does not lead to the possibility to write the scattering matrix in terms of
  known special functions}''; for $\eps_0\ne0$ ``\emph{there is likely no analytical solution of
  the general three-state LZ model}.'' Their working tool is the \textbf{Dykhne formula}
  $P=\eta\,e^{-2\,\Im\!\int\Delta E\,dt}$ with complex turning points where eigenvalues coincide
  (their Eqs.~41,46,47) --- \emph{this is exactly our $Q_4$-window construction}
  ($\delta_X=|\Im I_X|/2\pi$). Crucially they state there is \textbf{``no general analog of the
  Dykhne formula''} for $N{=}3$ and only ``limited progress'' on the prefactor $\eta$ and
  subdominant exponents. \emph{That prefactor/subdominant content is precisely our missing
  $\Pmid$ and off-diagonal.} They do give two exact handles: the $\eps_0=0$ slice is solvable
  (confluent hypergeometric; $P_{m\to m}(\eps{=}0)=2e^{-\pi g^2/b}/(1+e^{-\pi g^2/b})$, their
  Eq.~49) and the adiabatic limit is asymptotically exact via time-scale separation.
\item \textbf{Malikis--Cheianov 2025 (arXiv:2505.06048) --- the elementary route.} A
  \emph{zero-curvature} operator $\hat E$ ($\partial_\eps\hat H-\partial_t\hat E+i[\hat E,\hat H]=0$)
  lets one \textbf{deform the path in the $(t,\eps)$ plane} keeping endpoints fixed, so the
  S-matrix is path-independent. Deforming so that level crossings become \emph{well separated}
  reduces the exact $S$ to a \textbf{product of two-level LZ S-matrices}, e.g.\ for the
  $3$-level bow-tie $S_{BT}=S_{13}S_{23}$ (their Eqs.~28--29) --- \emph{elementary}. This is the
  rigorous statement of \emph{when} an ``incoherent product'' becomes exact: when an $\hat E$
  exists \emph{and} the path can be deformed to separate all crossings.
\item \textbf{BBGY 2024 (arXiv:2409.17053) --- KZ, but hyperbolic.} Integrable
  \emph{hyperbolic} LZ $\hat H=\hat A+\hat B/t\leftrightarrow$ KZ equations; the $N{=}3$ tunnelling
  amplitudes are a \textbf{two-variable Kamp\'e de F\'eriet} function (App.~A.2), the $2\times2$
  a Gauss hypergeometric. Not the linear model; tells us the \emph{expected transcendentality} of
  the generic off-diagonal, not an elementary formula.
\item \textbf{Lin--Sinitsyn 2013 (arXiv:1310.7245) --- the template.} Full $N{=}3$ LZ--Coulomb
  matrix: entries out of the special (Coulomb) level are \emph{elementary/rational} in the LZ
  factors, while transitions between the two ordinary diabatic levels are \emph{hypergeometric}
  with multiple oscillations. Their closing line --- ``the simplicity of known exact results
  \emph{does not directly follow from integrability}; there is an additional, yet unknown,
  principle'' --- is the precise warning against expecting elementary $P$ from integrability alone.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Reset of the practicality bar (honest).} Two of the four papers, written by the
architects of MLZ integrability and treating \emph{exactly} our model, indicate that a fully
elementary closed form for generic ($\eps_0\ne0$) Type-1 $N{=}3$ \textbf{very likely does not
exist} --- the missing $\Pmid$/off-diagonal is the \emph{multistate-Dykhne prefactor problem},
open with ``limited progress'' for $\sim$two decades. The realistic, still-publishable target set
is therefore: (a) the exact elementary entries (BE survivals + the ``out-of-special-level''
elements, à la Lin--Sinitsyn Table~2); (b) the \emph{exact} $\eps_0=0$ slice; (c) the
adiabatic-limit asymptotically-exact full $P$ (Dykhne + path deformation); and (d) --- the
home run --- an \emph{exact} factorised $S$ \emph{iff} a zero-curvature $\hat E$ exists for Type-1
and separates the crossings. The tournament below is re-ranked around this reset.

\paragraph{Addendum v2.1 (two structural findings, with code in \texttt{experiments/}).}
\emph{(a) The C--S solvable slice is outside Type-1.} The exact $\eps_0{=}0$ result (their Eq.~49)
requires \emph{both} a flat middle level \emph{and} zero outer--outer coupling $g_{\rm out}=0$. The
Type-1 dictionary maps $H_0+uA$ into the C--S frame exactly, but $g_{\rm out}=\gamma_{\rm lo}
\gamma_{\rm hi}(a_{\rm lo}-a_{\rm hi})/(\eps_{\rm lo}-\eps_{\rm hi})\ne0$ \emph{always} (couplings
never vanish), so Eq.~49 is \emph{not} a Type-1 anchor; S2's exact-slice claim is corrected to
``BE entries + deep-adiabatic only.'' \emph{(b) The commuting partner has an Abelian ceiling.} The
Type-1 family is a commutative ring: the time-quadratic commuting partner reduces to the linear
family with linear-in-$u$ coefficients ($H(u)^2=\sum_k c_k(u)H^{(a_k)}(u)$, verified $10^{-13}$),
and the whole family \emph{shares one eigenbasis} $\phi_i(u)$, differing only in the $a$-linear
eigenvalues. Hence a commuting partner yields \emph{only} Abelian data (spectrum $\to$ BE/Dykhne;
$\tau$-invariance) and \emph{cannot} reach the middle-survival prefactor --- which the anchor run
shows \emph{dominates} ($P_{\rm mid}/P_{\rm mid}^{\rm inc}=2.5$--$104\times$). Constructively, the
ring recasts $P^{(a)}$ as the holonomy of a \emph{fixed} algebraic connection
$W_{ij}=\langle\phi_i|\phi_j'\rangle$ ($a$-independent) twisted by $a$-linear phases --- an
isomonodromy/KZ object (Kamp\'e de F\'eriet), \emph{not} elementary. \textbf{This relocates the home
run: S1's zero-curvature $\hat E$ must be \emph{non-commuting} ($[\hat E,H]\ne0$), outside the ring;
its existence for the Cauchy/Gaudin (non-spin-rep) Type-1 family is the single decisive open
question.}

\section{Ranked strategies (v2, post-reading)}
%==============================================================
Each strategy lists: idea; why it can meet the practicality bar; near-term target; falsification
test (against the existing $10^{-9}$ harness).

\subsection*{[S1 --- TOP] Zero-curvature / Lax path-deformation (Malikis--Cheianov)}
\textbf{Idea.} Find the zero-curvature operator $\hat E$ for Type-1 $N{=}3$ (the commuting-partner
lever in $(t,\eps)$ form), then deform the evolution path so the three avoided crossings separate;
the exact S-matrix factorises into two-level LZ factors $S=\prod_{i<j}S_{ij}$, elementary in
$\{\gamma,\eps,a\}$.\\
\textbf{Practicality.} \emph{Highest} --- the only one of the four methods with demonstrated
elementary closed-form output, and it directly realises the user's commuting-partner steer. It
also \emph{explains} the project's $15\%$ ``incoherent-grid works'' observation: those are the
parameter regions where the deformed path already separates the crossings.\\
\textbf{Near-term target.} (i) Construct $\hat E$ from the Chernyak--Sinitsyn time-quadratic
partner (the two are the same integrability datum). (ii) Determine whether the constant middle
level $\eps_0$ obstructs full separation (Chernyak--Sinitsyn warn it can): does the bow-tie
factorisation survive, or does an irreducible $2{\times}3$ ``$C$ point'' remain (cf.\ BBGY Fig.~1)?\\
\textbf{Falsification.} Evaluate $S=\prod S_{ij}$ on the 8 benchmark strata to the $10^{-9}$ floor.
If the generic overlapping-crossing stratum misses by $>10^{-6}$, the path does \emph{not} fully
separate $\Rightarrow$ an irreducible interference factor survives (expected: the
multistate-Dykhne prefactor). That miss is itself the publishable boundary of factorisability.

\subsection*{[S2] Adiabatic Dykhne + exact $\eps_0{=}0$ slice (Chernyak--Sinitsyn)}
\textbf{Idea.} Recognise the project's $Q_4$-window machinery as the multistate Dykhne method;
take the BE survivals as its exact leading exponents, the $\eps_0=0$ confluent-hypergeometric
slice as an exact anchor (their Eq.~49), and develop the controlled adiabatic-limit expansion
(time-scale separation, their Sec.~III) for the leading behaviour of $\Pmid$.\\
\textbf{Practicality.} Mixed: the $\eps_0=0$ slice and BE entries are exact and elementary; the
generic $\Pmid$ comes out as an asymptotic series whose \emph{leading} term is closed-form but
whose prefactor is the hard part. This is the rigorous \emph{foundation}, not a full closed form.\\
\textbf{Near-term target.} (i) Reproduce Eq.~49 inside the harness as a zero-parameter check.
(ii) Compute the Dykhne exponent for the middle gap and compare to the benchmark $\Pmid$ across
strata --- quantify how much of $\Pmid$ is leading-exponent vs prefactor.\\
\textbf{Falsification.} If the leading Dykhne exponent already matches $\Pmid$ to $10^{-3}$ over a
stratum, the prefactor is mild there and a closed form is in reach; if it is off by $O(1)$, that
stratum needs the full (open) prefactor.

\subsection*{[S3] Contour-integral / KZ form of the off-diagonal (BBGY + Lin--Sinitsyn)}
\textbf{Idea.} Set up the Laplace/contour-integral solution of the $3\times3$ Cauchy system and
identify the off-diagonal's special-function class, using BBGY's KZ contour technology and
Lin--Sinitsyn's $N{=}3$ template as the expected answer (Kamp\'e de F\'eriet / Gauss
hypergeometric).\\
\textbf{Practicality.} Medium: yields an \emph{exact} but generally \emph{transcendental}
off-diagonal. Acceptable under a relaxed bar (``closed form in named special functions of
$\{\gamma,\eps,a\}$''), and decisive for confirming that the elementary bar cannot be met.\\
\textbf{Near-term target.} Transcribe the BBGY $N{=}3$ contour integral; attempt the
linear-in-$t$ limit (or the variable change relating $\hat A+\hat B/t$ to $\hat A+\hat Bt$) and
read off the off-diagonal's hypergeometric type.\\
\textbf{Falsification.} Benchmark gate; require BE entries to reappear as the elementary limit.

\subsection*{[S4 --- DEMOTED] KZ / Gaudin direct monodromy (BBGY)}
\textbf{Idea.} Read $P$ as the monodromy of the rational KZ/Gaudin flat connection, specialising
the BBGY $N{=}3$ solution.\\
\textbf{Why demoted (was \#1 in v1).} Now that the paper is read: BBGY is \emph{hyperbolic}
($\hat A+\hat B/t$), its $N{=}3$ amplitude is a two-variable Kamp\'e de F\'eriet function, and the
specialisation to linear $\hat A+\hat Bt$ is the same singular limit that makes the Coulomb models
non-elementary. It informs the \emph{transcendentality class} (subsumed into S3) but does not, on
its own evidence, deliver an elementary $P$.\\
\textbf{Residual use.} As the conceptual home of S3's contour integrals and as a cross-check that
any candidate off-diagonal has the right monodromy/branching.

\subsection*{[S5 --- finishing / LOW leverage for closed form] Complete the transport recurrence}
\textbf{Idea.} Close $\Theta^{\rm tr}_v(t)$ to all orders by the same Lagrange--B\"urmann route
that closed the phase side, then test for a residue/period collapse of its selector value on
$\nu^2=W_4$.\\
\textbf{Practicality.} \emph{Only} practical \emph{if} the selector value collapses to residues
(like $I_X$ did); as an unresummed series it fails the bar. Even then it finishes the
local-block programme, not the global amplitude.\\
\textbf{Near-term target.} Derive $K_5,K_7$; test whether
$\Theta^{\rm tr}_v(t_\star)$ collapses to a finite period sum.\\
\textbf{Falsification.} If $K_{2m+1}$ shows no collapse pattern by $m=3$, deprioritize. (Useful
regardless as it completes the exact-WKB paper's stated open item, but it is not the closed-form
lever --- see \S2.)

\noindent\textbf{Revised critical path.}
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,leftmargin=1.4em]
\item \textbf{Days 1--2 (cheap, decisive anchors).} Reproduce the Chernyak--Sinitsyn $\eps_0=0$
  exact result (Eq.~49) inside the harness as a zero-parameter validation, and overlay the leading
  Dykhne exponent for the middle gap against the benchmark $\Pmid$ on all 8 strata. This
  \emph{measures} how much of $\Pmid$ is leading-exponent vs.\ the hard prefactor --- telling us
  immediately where (if anywhere) an elementary form is reachable.
\item \textbf{Days 3--8 (S1, the home-run attempt).} Construct the zero-curvature $\hat E$ from the
  time-quadratic commuting partner; deform the path and test whether the constant middle level
  $\eps_0$ permits full crossing separation into $S=\prod S_{ij}$, or leaves an irreducible
  $2{\times}3$ interference vertex. Either outcome is a result: an exact factorised $S$, \emph{or}
  a sharp characterisation of the non-factorisable (multistate-Dykhne-prefactor) remainder.
\item \textbf{Days 9--14 (falsify + bound).} Benchmark every candidate to $10^{-9}$. Where S1
  factorises, prove it (zero-curvature + separation $\Rightarrow$ exact product); where it does
  not, use S3/S4 to name the transcendental class of the residual off-diagonal and publish the
  \emph{factorisability boundary} as the contribution.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Highest-conviction single move.} Construct the zero-curvature operator $\hat E$ for
Type-1 $N{=}3$ (equivalently, recast the known time-quadratic commuting partner in $(t,\eps)$
form) and test whether path deformation separates all three avoided crossings. If it does, $P$ is
an \emph{exact, elementary} product of two-level S-matrices --- the home run, directly built on the
commuting-partner structure. If the constant middle level obstructs separation, the surviving
factor is exactly the multistate-Dykhne prefactor the literature flags as open, and bounding it is
itself the publishable result.

\paragraph{Honest caveats (v2).} (i) Full texts are now read; the central new fact is the
authors' own verdict that generic $\eps_0\ne0$ Type-1 $N{=}3$ ``\emph{likely has no analytical

what spoils clean reduction --- so this is genuinely open and worth the first real effort.
